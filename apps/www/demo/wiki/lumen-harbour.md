**Lumen Harbour** occupies a narrow inlet beneath [[Beacon Ridge]]. A curved stone wall breaks the swell before it reaches the landing steps. At low tide the wall seems to stand in a field of dark weed; at high tide only its pale coping shows above the water.

The harbour is the meeting point of the island paths and the inner-sea crossing. Arrivals read the coloured boards beside the steps before choosing either route.[^boards]

## Arrival

The upper steps are the usual landing. The lower flight is reserved for shallow boats near the turn of the tide. Neither landing is used while the channel bell is sounding: that signal means the ferry is already entering the inlet.

### By water

[[Reed Light]] marks the last bend. Keep its steady amber light in view until the harbour wall opens to the right. The [[Tide calendar]] explains the local names used on the landing boards.

## Harbour life

A covered table beside the steps holds the day's crossing notes. Walkers heading uphill leave the quay by the rain cistern and join the path to [[Crown Light]]. The ridge and the light are both sometimes called [[Crown]], a useful distinction when asking for directions.

The shore route towards [[Glasswater Sound]] has no guide entry yet. Survey notes for that inlet are still being collected.

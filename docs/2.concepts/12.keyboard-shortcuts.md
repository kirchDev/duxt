The theme binds four keys. This page is what they are, when they are listening,
and where a reader who has never read this page finds out about them — because a
shortcut nobody can discover is a shortcut nobody has.

## The bindings

| Keys                                                    | Does                                    | Listening while                                          |
| :------------------------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- |
| <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> · <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> | Opens search, and closes it again       | Anywhere on the page, a focused link or button included    |
| <kbd>?</kbd>                                            | Opens the shortcut sheet                | The page's own content holds focus                         |
| <kbd>[</kbd>                                            | The previous page **of this section**   | The page's own content holds focus                         |
| <kbd>]</kbd>                                            | The next page **of this section**       | The page's own content holds focus                         |

The modifier follows the reader's keyboard, not the author's: `⌘` on an Apple
one, `Ctrl` everywhere else. It is resolved once the browser is there to answer,
so a prerendered page ships the safer `Ctrl` and swaps on arrival.

`[` and `]` stay inside the section being read. The last page of one section
offers no next page into another — crossing a section is what the section row is
for, and a key called "next page" that leaves the subject is not the same key.

## Where the reader is told

Four places, and all four draw from the same definition, so none of them can
come to advertise a key the layer has stopped binding:

- **The search button** carries its own chord — `⌘K` or `Ctrl+K` — in the cap on
  its right.
- **The previous and next links** at the foot of a page carry `[` and `]`. These
  were the keys nothing mentioned: a reader had to open the sheet to learn that
  the two links they were already looking at had keys.
- **The keyboard button** beside the search opens the sheet with a pointer, on
  every page and at every width. It is the answer to the circular part of the
  problem — `?` opens the list of keys, and you have to know that to press it.
- **The sheet itself** lists every live binding with its keys, on `?` or on that
  button.

## When a key stands down

A global single-character shortcut fires under a reader who never opted into it,
so the unmodified keys are guarded rather than merely bound. Nothing fires:

- once something nearer the key has already handled it;
- while the reader is typing — an input, a textarea, a select, a contenteditable
  region;
- anywhere inside a dialog, menu, listbox, combobox, grid or tree, which answer
  keystrokes themselves.

The unmodified keys stand down once further: they fire only while the page's own
content holds focus, never while a link, button or tab does. `⌘/Ctrl+K` is the
exception, because no control wants that chord.

A page focus target with `tabindex="-1"` counts as content, deliberately — that
is where focus parks after a navigation, and a reader who has just landed on a
page is exactly who `[` and `]` are for.

## Turning the bare keys off

`shortcuts.singleCharacter: false` unbinds `?`, `[` and `]` and leaves
`⌘/Ctrl+K`. Switch access, voice control and dictation all emit bare characters
as a matter of course, and a page that navigates away under one of them shows no
cause; no input method produces the modified chord by accident.

::callout{type="warning" title="It unbinds the help key too"}
`?` is a bare character like the others, so a site that sets this has no key that
opens the shortcut sheet. The keyboard button in the header is then the only way
in — which is why it is drawn at every width and never given a responsive class
of its own. Remove it from an overridden header and the sheet becomes
unreachable.
::

The policy is a boolean rather than a binding table, and that is deliberate: see
[`shortcuts` in the configuration reference](/reference/configuration).

## Overriding any of it

The list, the guards and the drawing are one module —
[`useDuxtShortcuts()`](/reference/composables/reading/use-duxt-shortcuts). The
two components around it are
[`DuxtShortcuts`](/reference/components/controls/duxt-shortcuts), the sheet, and
[`DuxtShortcutsTrigger`](/reference/components/controls/duxt-shortcuts-trigger),
the button that opens it. Both are yours to replace the ordinary way; see
[Override the theme](/guides/override-the-theme).
